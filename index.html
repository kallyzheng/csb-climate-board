2 · How to do it

  1. Pick a company, not a posting. There is nothing to apply to — that is the advantage. You arrive before the ad exists, and before it draws hundreds of applicants.
  2. Use the Pitch Route column. Cold Email means a real address and no form. Open Application means the company has explicitly invited exactly this, so start there if you are new to cold outreach.
  3. Lead with the work, not your CV. Name one problem you would own in the first ninety days — a pricing analysis, a channel test, a supplier comparison — and why it matters at their size. “Do you have any openings?” gets nothing; “here is the gap I think you have, and how I would close it” gets a reply.
  4. Show you did the reading. Reference their raise, their product, their market. The Notion row gives you sector, headcount, total raised and weeks since raise before you have opened a single tab.
  5. Keep it to five sentences and one ask. Suggest a 20-minute call. Attach nothing unless asked. Follow up once, about ten days later, and then stop.

3 · Reading how a company is scaling

Under 10
No functions yet — the founders still do everything. You are pitching to fill the gap they feel most acutely. Breadth beats specialism here, and the work is undefined by nature.
10–25
The first functional hires appear: someone owning go-to-market, someone owning operations. If a function you know is missing from their team page, that absence is your opening.
25–50
Process starts to break. Pricing, forecasting, hiring plans and partnerships get real owners around here — the classic MBA entry points.
50+
Structure exists and roles get posted properly. These companies mostly show up on the Job board tab instead.

A shortcut: open their team page and count functions, not people. A company with eight engineers and nobody owning go-to-market is telling you exactly what it needs next.

You may be able to bring your own funding. Past projects have covered strategy, marketing, business development, operations and financial modelling. That is a strong opening line for a nine-person team with a real problem and no headcount budget. Employers post opportunities via sternworks@stern.nyu.edu.
